First, a ray is fully defined by its origin point o and its direction d. Any point along the ray corresponds to a value of a scalar parameter t and can be found by evaluating o + t*d.

O = starting point
D = the direction itself
T = how many units youve moved

That returns the point in space of where you currently are, based upon T (well, obviously o and d are also important, but you -- I -- get the idea).


An AABB can be represented by 3 "slabs"
    A slab is essentially 2 infinite parallel planes.
    A AABB is a rectangular shape, and so has 3 parallel "slabs" (planes).  this includes:
        the 2 X planes (the sides of the bounding box) -- slab 1
        the 2 Y planes (the top of the bounding box) -- slab 2
        the 2 Z planes (the front and back of the bounding box) -- slab 3
    Due to the nature of these being INFINITE parallel planes to a rectangle, these slabs are intersecting each other.
        https://www.desmos.com/3d/45u8z6saep For a visual.

Separating Axis Theorem

extent.x * std::abs(frustumPlanes[0].normal.x)

the NORMAL.x is JUST the X direction of the frustum: 1.0 means its pointing PERFECTLY on the X axis, 0.5 means its slanted halfway, and 0.0 means its not at all moving towards X.
Multiplying the extent of X by this value gives the number of "UNITS" that this box's X is visible -- think of it as a percentage:
If the normal.x is 1.0, it means we see the ENTIRETY of the full box, hence it returns extent.X
However, if the normal is 0.5, then we only see half of extent.x (so extent.x * 0.5) -- if the frustum is NOT moving AT ALL on the X axis (0.0), then we see NOTHING.
You then do this for ALL axis, add them up to get an effective visiblity (r)
If the DISTANCE from the AABB box's centre is GREATER than that effective visiblity value (-r), it is NOT visible.

Think of it like REACH

In 1 DIMENSION:

If pointX is 10 units away from the plane, then its REACH has to be 10 or more to be visible.
'R' is a LITERAL radius around the centre point.
